## Final abstract

Recursive graph bisection offers an intelligible construction rule for
redistricting, but practical partitioners ordinarily return heuristic cuts
without proving that a better permitted cut does not exist. It therefore
remains unresolved whether a fixed bisection schedule can support both
model-scoped optimality evidence and staged public scrutiny without implying
that its governing objective is uniquely fair. We present certified recursive
bisection, a proof-carrying architecture that separates institutional choice
of a rule from verifiable execution. The procedure freezes the unit universe,
population, adjacency, district-count tree, lexicographic objective, and
canonical tie-break; reconstructs child instances from certified parent
assignments; and binds all internal cuts to one-seat leaves. A public ceremony
commits the rule before disclosure and releases certified cuts by tree depth
after fixed review intervals. On eight precommitted synthetic graphs and five
METIS seeds, heuristic cuts match the exact primary objective in 30 of 40 rows
and the exact canonical assignment in 20. In a four-district ceremony fixture,
all three internal cuts are released across two rounds, the final tree binds,
and a timestamp mutation is rejected. Rhode Island demonstrates a 25,649-block
population-proof frontier. A clean-worktree replay exactly regenerates all 150
State-cycle assignments across three national operational baselines, whose
1,155 recursive nodes are within tolerance but provide zero State-scale
weighted-boundary or canonical proofs. These results supply a concrete
evaluation contract: future bakeoffs can report map outcomes and procedural
evidence separately, while legislatures and commissions retain responsibility
for legal, representational, and normative choices.
