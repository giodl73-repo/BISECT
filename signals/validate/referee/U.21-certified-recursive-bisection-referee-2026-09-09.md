# Hostile referee simulation: U.21

Central claim: certified recursive bisection supplies a model-scoped proof and
publication architecture, not a universally fair map. Most likely divergent
referee precommitment: Referee 2, the optimization-proof skeptic, is most likely
to recommend rejection because no unrestricted State boundary or canonical
proof has completed, whereas the other referees can accept a bounded methods
paper after revisions.

## Referee 1 — redistricting-methods specialist

Recommendation: Major Revision

The separation of rule selection from execution is useful and the two-scoreboard
proposal is unusually disciplined. The institutional case remains incomplete
without a representative legal-constraint profile or evidence about how public
review changes the process.

Major concerns:

- I-01 (P2): The frozen objective omits VRA, communities, and subdivision
  constraints; the manuscript disclaims them but needs one worked extension
  showing that certification survives a legally richer feasible set.
- I-02 (P2): The “Huntington--Hill analogy” is architectural, not an axiomatic
  characterization. State explicitly that no uniqueness theorem selects this
  districting rule among alternatives.
- I-03 (P2): The ceremony models challenge timing but not challenge adjudication.
  Define who decides whether a challenge halts a run and what evidence suffices.
- I-04 (P2): Depth batching is plausible, but no public-review study supports
  the chosen interval or demonstrates that observers can evaluate a cut in time.
- I-05 (P3): The paper should show the two procedural fixture rows in a small
  table rather than describing them only in prose.

Specific comment: Section 3, ceremony — distinguish cryptographic finality from
administrative finality after a successful challenge.

## Referee 2 — optimization and proof skeptic

Recommendation: Reject in present form; invite resubmission as a methods note

The bounded verifier is persuasive, but the headline architecture is much
further from unrestricted State-scale certification than the title may suggest.
Population optimality in Rhode Island does not establish boundary or canonical
optimality, and all national boundary/canonical coverage is zero.

Major concerns:

- I-06 (P2): No theorem or checker-independent argument establishes soundness
  and completeness of the parent-depth connectivity encoding in the manuscript.
- I-07 (P2): The paper does not report model-generation time, proof-generation
  time, proof size, or verifier memory for the bounded comparison in one table.
- I-08 (P2): Rhode Island boundary branches are unsearched or timed out. The
  practical gap from population proof to complete root certification is unknown.
- I-09 (P2): Sequential local optimality can strand a descendant. The paper
  declares failure but does not quantify its frequency or compare with a
  globally feasible tree formulation.
- I-10 (P2): “Strongest model-optimality claim architecture currently
  implemented in the project” is an internal comparison and should not be used
  as an external novelty claim.
- I-11 (P2): The 24-unit exact-oracle ceiling and synthetic graph selection
  limit generalization; add adversarial graph families or an explicit scope
  theorem.

Specific comment: Section 4 — state exactly which accepted VeriPB proof is
bounded and prevent readers from associating it with the timed-out RI boundary.

The ceremony fixture and 40-row comparison regenerate. The national Level-1

- I-14 (P2): Receipt strings are syntactic witnesses only. Implement at least

- I-16 (P3): Record the exact binary hash and environment alongside the ceremony
  scoreboard rather than accepting a caller-supplied binary without a manifest
  binding.

Specific comment: Section 5 correctly distinguishes the completed local
clean-worktree replay from a future independent second-machine witness.
