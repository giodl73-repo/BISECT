# Model Federal Statute: Districting Integrity And Disclosure Act

**Status:** v0.2 candidate, 2026-07-10
**Audience:** congressional staff, commissions, civil-rights counsel, election
administrators, and academic reviewers
**Technical baseline:** `docs/specs/2026-07-09-national-redistricting-standard-v0.1.md`
**Claim posture:** Model text only; not enacted law or legal advice

## Architecture

This version does not require a State to adopt the algorithmic benchmark as its
final congressional map. It requires:

1. a fixed constitutional and data floor;
2. publication of a precommitted, partisan-input-excluded geographic benchmark;
3. publication of the State's final plan;
4. a machine-readable baseline-to-final diff and public reasons;
5. mandatory compliance with federal voting-rights law; and
6. reproducible, challengeable artifacts.

The benchmark is evidence. It is not a safe harbor, a uniquely fair map, or a
substitute for the final legal decision maker.

---

## Title

**Districting Integrity and Disclosure Act of [Year]**

Long title: *An Act to establish national reproducibility, disclosure, and
review standards for congressional redistricting.*

## Section 101. Findings and purposes

Congress finds that:

1. Article I, section 4 authorizes Congress to make or alter regulations
   governing congressional elections, including districting requirements.
2. Congress has previously required single-member congressional districts and
   population equality.
3. *Rucho v. Common Cause* left partisan-gerrymandering reform to Congress and
   the States while rejecting an open-ended federal constitutional test.
4. A reproducible benchmark can expose hidden line-drawing discretion without
   requiring Congress or a court to declare one partisan metric constitutionally
   controlling.
5. Exclusion of partisan or racial data from benchmark execution does not prove
   that a plan is fair or lawful and does not displace federal voting-rights
   obligations.
6. Public modifications, alternatives, and reasons are necessary because
   algorithm structure, geographic resolution, data preparation, and legal
   exceptions can affect representational outcomes.

The purposes of this Act are to:

- establish a national evidence and disclosure floor;
- make benchmark generation independently reproducible;
- preserve lawful State, commission, and judicial responsibility for final
  congressional plans;
- require public justification of benchmark departures; and
- provide manageable procedures for technical challenge and correction.

## Section 102. Definitions

1. **BLOCK** means a decennial census block identified by its 15-character
   GEOID, or a finer legally controlling population unit designated by Congress.
2. **BLOCK POPULATION** means the resident population assigned to a BLOCK in
   the controlling decennial census release, subject only to numbered official
   corrections.
3. **BLOCK ADJACENCY PROFILE** means the versioned national rule identifying
   geographic adjacency, including treatment of water, islands, enclaves,
   point contacts, slivers, zero-population blocks, and documented bridges.
4. **BENCHMARK PROFILE** means the assignment-affecting rules enacted in
   Section 104.
5. **GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK** means the assignment produced by executing the
   BENCHMARK PROFILE. It is a comparison artifact and is not automatically the
   final congressional plan.
6. **FINAL PLAN** means the congressional assignment adopted by the legally
   authorized State body or court.
7. **MODIFICATION RECORD** means the machine-readable and human-readable record
   required by Section 106.
8. **CANONICAL ASSIGNMENT HASH** means SHA-256 of the assignment under
   `canonical-json-v1`: UTF-8; object keys sorted lexicographically; arrays
   preserving order; no insignificant whitespace; base-10 integers without
   leading zeros; finite shortest-roundtrip floats; and absent fields distinct
   from explicit `null`.
9. **REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATION** means the open-source implementation and
   conformance fixtures maintained under Section 108.
10. **ASSIGNMENT-AFFECTING CHANGE** means a change to structure, weights, seed
    derivation, population rules, adjacency, engine, unit resolution, or any
    other rule capable of changing the benchmark assignment.

## Section 103. Duties of each State

For each congressional redistricting cycle, a State shall:

1. generate the GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK using the REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATION or
   obtain a benchmark generated by the federal reference service;
2. publish the benchmark and required artifacts before adopting the FINAL PLAN;
3. conduct a public process under applicable State and federal law;
4. adopt a FINAL PLAN satisfying controlling constitutional and statutory law;
5. publish a MODIFICATION RECORD comparing the benchmark and FINAL PLAN; and
6. preserve the required source and output artifacts for not less than 12 years.

The State is not required to enact the GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK as the FINAL PLAN.
Failure to enact the benchmark creates no presumption for or against the FINAL
PLAN if the State satisfies this Act.

## Section 104. Geographic benchmark profile

### (a) Structure

Technical Schedule A (`NRS_TECHNICAL_SCHEDULE_V0.1.md`) is incorporated into
this model Act as the candidate BENCHMARK PROFILE.

The benchmark shall use standard recursive bisection. At each node containing
`k` remaining districts, the left and right child receive `floor(k/2)` and
`ceil(k/2)` districts under a fixed ordering rule.

Prime-factor, ratio-optimal, county-sticky, proportional, VRA-aligned, and other
profiles are research or comparison profiles and are not the statutory
benchmark.

### (b) Inputs

The benchmark shall use only:

- the controlling BLOCK universe;
- BLOCK POPULATION;
- the BLOCK ADJACENCY PROFILE;
- the State's apportioned congressional district count;
- the enacted BENCHMARK PROFILE; and
- the pinned REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATION identity.

Election results, registration, party affiliation, candidate identity,
incumbency, race, ethnicity, language, and community-of-interest evidence shall
not enter benchmark generation.

### (c) Objective and enacted technical schedule

Edge weight shall equal shared geographic-boundary length under the controlling
TIGER/Line vintage and adjacency profile. The benchmark shall execute the
enacted reference procedure for weighted-cut reduction subject to population
and connectivity constraints. It does not claim a global optimum.

The enacted BENCHMARK PROFILE or an incorporated technical schedule shall fix:

- Census and TIGER release identifiers and hashes;
- block adjacency and edge-length calculation;
- recursive ordering and district-label tie breaks;
- population constraints and infeasibility behavior;
- canonicalization and seed conversion;
- reference-engine source identity and hash;
- deterministic output ordering; and
- positive and negative conformance fixtures.

TIGER geometry is an input to boundary-length calculation and shall be included
in the canonical input manifest.

### (d) Search and seed

The benchmark shall execute one seed derived as:

```text
SHA-256("NRS_BASELINE_V0_1" || canonical_input_manifest_bytes)
```

The engine seed shall be the unsigned 64-bit little-endian integer represented
by digest bytes 0 through 7. An engine requiring a narrower signed seed shall
use the conversion enacted in the technical schedule. Fixtures test the rule;
they do not define or amend it. The seed schema and every seed-affecting input
rule shall be fixed before release of the redistricting census data.

### (e) Population and contiguity

The benchmark shall:

- assign every BLOCK exactly once;
- produce the legally apportioned district count;
- produce contiguous districts under the BLOCK ADJACENCY PROFILE; and
- achieve population equality as nearly exact as practicable.

A configured optimizer tolerance is not a legal safe harbor. The benchmark
record shall report ideal population, maximum and total deviation, and whether
a lower-deviation assignment was found under the same profile.

### (f) Engine

Congress shall enact the benchmark method and assignment-affecting profile.
The reference-engine source identity and hash are part of that enacted profile.
NIST is the technical custodian. It may certify a defect patch only after the published
fixture suite demonstrates preservation of every canonical reference
assignment. Any engine, seed conversion, or patch capable of changing an
assignment requires an Act of Congress.

## Section 105. Federal and constitutional floor

The FINAL PLAN shall:

1. contain the legally apportioned number of single-member districts;
2. assign the entire population exactly once;
3. contain contiguous districts under the controlling legal adjacency rule;
4. satisfy population equality under controlling Supreme Court precedent;
5. comply with the Voting Rights Act and the Constitution; and
6. comply with controlling State law except where valid federal law preempts it.

Conformance of the GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK does not establish conformance of the
FINAL PLAN, and benchmark failure cannot be cured by favorable political or
demographic outcomes.

## Section 106. Final-plan modifications and public record

### (a) Mandatory grounds

The FINAL PLAN shall be modified only to the extent required by:

- the Constitution;
- the Voting Rights Act or other federal voting-rights law; or
- a court order.

For Voting Rights Act analysis, the GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK is not the appropriate
vote-dilution benchmark rejected in *Allen v. Milligan*. It is not evidence
that Section 2 has been satisfied, is not a defense to discriminatory purpose
or effect, and may not be used to rebut a *Gingles* or totality showing.

### (b) Authorized grounds

A FINAL PLAN may differ from the benchmark under:

- controlling State constitutional or statutory criteria;
- a community-of-interest criterion published and locked before the benchmark
  or any candidate plan is generated;
- correction of census, TIGER, topology, adjacency, or assignment error; or
- another authority identified in the published legal profile.

A community criterion shall have a public evidentiary definition, a published
conflict-resolution hierarchy, and a disparate-impact review. Good-faith
recognition of a racial or language-minority community supported by shared
interests is permitted. The anti-proxy rule prohibits pretextual partisan,
dilutive, candidate, or incumbency selection; it does not prohibit recognition
of minority communities.

### (c) Record

The MODIFICATION RECORD shall identify:

- legal authority;
- evidence considered;
- all moved BLOCKS and population;
- affected districts;
- alternatives considered;
- for an authorized State-law, COI, or correction change, at least one
  reproducible legally permissible lower-departure alternative;
- the precommitted departure measures, including blocks moved, population
  moved, districts touched, subdivision/community effects, and minority-
  opportunity delta;
- reasons the selected authorized change was proportionate to its stated
  purpose;
- effects on population, contiguity, compactness, subdivisions, communities,
  racial opportunity, and partisan diagnostics, where applicable;
- decision maker and vote;
- public-comment disposition; and
- canonical hashes of every referenced artifact.

No universal percentage cap applies to moved units. The legal authority,
alternatives, concentration, and effects of the change govern review.

Federally required changes under subsection (a) are exempt from any presumption
favoring proximity to the GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK. Their comparator is a lawful,
fully compliant alternative under controlling law. Additional lawful minority
opportunity is not disfavored merely because it moves more units from the
benchmark.

### (d) VRA record

A race-conscious change shall publicly identify the controlling authority and
legal conclusions required by current law. Underlying racially polarized
voting, intent, and narrow-tailoring expert material may be filed in a
protected or in-camera appendix available to reviewing courts, parties, and the
independent reviewer under protective order. Required disclosure is not itself
an admission that race predominated. A diagnostic majority-minority threshold
is not a legal conclusion.

Where race-conscious and State-law-authorized partisan considerations affect
overlapping districts or geography, the MODIFICATION RECORD shall separately
identify each authority, input, stage, decision maker, and effect and shall
address predominance under controlling law. Separate software runs alone do not
establish disentanglement. Racially polarized voting evidence used for VRA
analysis is not prohibited partisan optimization input.

### (e) Public process

The benchmark, proposed FINAL PLAN, proposed changes, community submissions,
and alternative maps shall receive at least 30 days of public comment and two
geographically accessible hearings before final adoption, except for an
emergency court order. Materials shall be machine-readable, accessible,
plain-language, and translated as required by Section 203 of the Voting Rights
Act and other applicable law.

Within 5 days after publication of the proposed FINAL PLAN, the Election
Assistance Commission shall fund and select a plan-specific
independent reviewer with access to public artifacts and protected evidence.
Reviewer qualification, financial conflict, prior representation, recusal, and
data-access rules shall be published before the cycle. The reviewer shall issue
findings within 30 days. The final authority shall publish a reasoned response
within 10 days and shall not adopt the plan before both documents are public.
Personal contact information, precise residence data, and protected expert
material shall be redacted or handled under a published privacy and protective-
order rule without suppressing the substance of community evidence.

## Section 107. Required artifacts

Each State shall publish:

1. `standard_profile.json`;
2. `legal_profile.json`;
3. `input_manifest.json`;
4. `unit_index.json`;
5. `baseline_assignments.json`;
6. `baseline_manifest.json`;
7. `final_assignments.json`;
8. `modification_record.json`;
9. `evaluation_manifest.json`;
10. `report_manifest.json`; and
11. `challenge_log.json`.

The State shall also publish an accessible plain-language and translated
summary describing the benchmark, final plan, major changes, community effects,
racial-opportunity findings, partisan diagnostics, and unresolved limitations.

The federal manifest registry shall specify schemas, canonical fields, verifier
paths, unknown-version rejection, and hash links. Existing RPLAN, RCTX, audit
certificate, provenance, analysis, and report formats shall be reused where
their versioned contracts apply.

Functional assignment equivalence is required. Byte-identical executable files
are not required unless a later reproducible-build profile expressly provides
for them.

## Section 107A. National evaluation profile

Technical Schedule B (`NRS_EVALUATION_SCHEDULE_V0.1.md`) is incorporated as the
candidate national reporting profile. Congress shall enact or incorporate a versioned national reporting profile
before the affected census release. The State shall lock any jurisdiction-
specific additions before benchmark or candidate-plan generation.

At minimum, the profile shall report:

- population deviation and contiguity;
- multiple geometric compactness measures;
- county, municipal, and subdivision splits;
- submitted community splits and dispositions;
- racial-opportunity and disparate-impact evaluation of both benchmark and
  FINAL PLAN;
- multiple partisan diagnostics across every election named in the profile;
- baseline-to-final changes;
- seed, resolution, profile, and engine sensitivity where available; and
- ensemble results only when traces, diagnostics, election inputs, and
  uncertainty are archived.

A data-unavailable exception shall identify the missing data, attempted source,
effect on interpretation, and correction plan. The authority may not select
metrics after observing which profile favors a preferred plan.

## Section 108. Administration and governance

### (a) Census Bureau

The Census Bureau shall publish the controlling census releases, geographic
vintages, BLOCK universe, source hashes, and numbered corrections.

### (b) NIST

NIST shall maintain conformance schemas, canonicalization fixtures, deterministic
build guidance, and technical testing of the REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATION.

### (c) Election Assistance Commission

The Election Assistance Commission is the lead administrative agency and the
federal reference service. It shall administer publication, public comment,
State and community technical assistance, independent review, challenge
intake, the federal manifest registry, and the standards register. It shall
publish implementing deadlines and dispositions required by this Act.

### (d) Advisory board

The Commission shall appoint a balanced advisory board including:

- graph-partitioning expertise;
- election geography;
- reproducible software;
- State election administration;
- Voting Rights Act and civil-rights expertise;
- community-of-interest and public-participation expertise; and
- members selected by each major party and by nonpartisan civic organizations.

The board advises; it may not amend assignment-affecting statutory rules.

## Section 109. Versioning, precommitment, and corrections

1. Every profile shall have a semantic version, effective census cycles,
   normative source identity, engine identity, schemas, fixtures, rationale,
   public-comment record, and migration statement.
2. An ASSIGNMENT-AFFECTING CHANGE shall be enacted before release of the
   redistricting census data for the affected cycle.
3. A completed cycle shall not be retroactively rerun except through a published
   defect or errata process preserving the original artifacts.
4. Any person may challenge source identity, input hashes, adjacency, topology,
   parameters, seed, determinism, incomplete diffs, unsupported evidence, or
   failure to disclose a required decision.
5. The Election Assistance Commission shall acknowledge a challenge within 10
   days and publish a disposition, reasons, corrected artifacts, and
   supersession status within 60 days, subject to judicial review.

Technical challenge findings do not decide VRA liability, constitutional
validity, discriminatory effect, or substantive fairness. Nothing in this
section limits a substantive federal or State claim.

## Section 110. Enforcement

### (a) Technical nonconformance

A State shall receive 30 days to cure a publication, hash, schema, or technical
execution defect that does not implicate voting-rights liability or an imminent
election.

### (b) Voting-rights claims

Nothing in this Act delays, narrows, preempts, or alters remedies under the
Voting Rights Act, Constitution, State constitution, State districting law,
community-of-interest law, or other substantive redistricting authority.
Procedural compliance with this Act is not a defense to any substantive federal
or State claim.

### (c) Cause of action

An affected voter, candidate, political party, commission, or organization may
seek declaratory and injunctive relief only upon showing concrete,
particularized injury caused by noncompliance, including dilution of the
plaintiff's vote, competitive injury, denial of participation or information
required for an imminent districting decision, or diversion of organizational
resources. A bare procedural violation alone does not confer standing.

### (d) Remedy

The ordinary remedy is publication, correction, reasoned reconsideration, or a
new lawful process. A court may select the GEOGRAPHIC BENCHMARK as an interim
plan only if it independently satisfies controlling law. Otherwise the most
recent lawfully effective plan remains temporarily in force, or the court may
order another lawful interim plan under existing equitable authority, subject
to election-proximity and voter-confusion considerations.

## Section 111. Constitutional and implementation postures

### (a) Primary posture: national disclosure floor

Congress enacts publication, reproducibility, and process requirements under
Article I, section 4. States retain final line-drawing authority subject to
federal and State law.

### (b) Direct federal backstop

If a State does not publish a benchmark within 180 days after release of the
redistricting block data, the Election Assistance Commission shall generate
and publish the benchmark evidence package within 30 additional days. The
service does not adopt the FINAL PLAN.

### (c) Preemption

A congressional plan adopted without the artifacts and decision record required
by this Act is not effective after the 30-day cure period in Section 110(a).
Where Section 110(a) excludes cure because voting-rights liability or an
imminent election is implicated, the reviewing court shall set the shortest
lawful cure schedule consistent with the governing substantive right. The most recent lawfully
effective plan remains temporarily in force, or a court shall select a lawful
interim plan, until a conforming process is completed. No remedy shall be
ordered so near an election that it creates greater voter confusion than the
violation being remedied unless voting-rights law requires immediate relief.

### (d) Commission support

Congress authorizes grants, fee waivers, and technical assistance to State
commissions, courts, election offices, community organizations, and qualified
challengers that execute or review the benchmark and public process.

### (e) Conditional-funding alternative

If a court holds that direct procedural duties impermissibly compel State
officers, those duties and the preemption penalty enforcing them are severed
together. Congress separately offers a voluntary condition on prospective Help
America Vote Act or successor election-administration grants designated for
redistricting data, publication, or voter information. The condition shall be
unambiguous, germane to the funded program, limited to prospective funds, and
noncoercive; declining the funds shall not reduce unrelated existing funding.

## Section 112. Effective date and transition

1. The Act applies beginning with the first decennial redistricting cycle whose
   census release occurs at least 30 months after enactment.
2. The first benchmark profile, schemas, fixtures, and technical-assistance
   program shall be published at least 24 months before that release.
   The Act is not effective until NIST certifies a block-level reference
   implementation, real-State conformance fixture, federal reference service,
   reviewer program, and federal manifest registry.
3. A State shall publish its proposed final plan and complete record at least
   120 days before the first primary using the plan, unless a court finds that
   census delay or emergency makes that deadline impossible.
4. Courts may extend publication deadlines for census delay, declared
   emergency, or implementation defect, but shall preserve public review to the
   maximum practicable extent.

## Section 113. Severability

Except as provided in Section 111(e), invalidation of one provision does not
affect the remainder. The benchmark-publication duty, final-plan duty,
preemption remedy, and direct State process duties shall be severed as a group
when the constitutional defect found by a court applies to their combined
operation. Substantive federal and State voting-rights savings clauses remain
effective.

## Section 114. Authorization and appropriations

There are authorized and appropriated $250,000,000 for each decennial
redistricting cycle:

- $60,000,000 for the EAC federal reference service and registry;
- $80,000,000 for State, commission, court, and election-office grants;
- $50,000,000 for community organizations, qualified challengers, language
  access, and fee waivers;
- $40,000,000 for independent plan reviewers; and
- $20,000,000 for NIST conformance, security, and reproducible-build work.

Acceptance of a grant under this new program is voluntary. The grant notice
shall state every condition before acceptance. No unrelated existing federal
funds are reduced for declining the program.
