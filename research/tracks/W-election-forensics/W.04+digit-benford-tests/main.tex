\begin{abstract}
Digit-distribution tests compare reported election counts to simple reference
patterns: first-digit Benford frequencies, second-digit frequencies,
last-digit uniformity, and related distance or chi-square summaries. This paper
places those tests inside the W-series forensic report contract defined by the
RCOUNT election-forensics overview. The boundary is deliberately strict. Digit
and Benford-style tests can prioritize investigation, but they cannot prove
fraud, certify correctness, or replace risk-limiting audits. They are also among
the weakest forensic screens for election counts. Precinct totals often have
bounded ranges and similar magnitudes, so the assumptions behind Benford's law
are commonly absent; deviations are expected and weakly diagnostic. The method
defined here treats digit tests as low-weight, caveated anomaly screens over
hash-bound RCOUNT summaries and large count tables, with fixtures that include
both planted digit manipulation and innocent failures caused by precinct-size
structure.
\end{abstract}
