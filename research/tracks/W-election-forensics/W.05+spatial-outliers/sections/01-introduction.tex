\section{Introduction}

Forensic spatial-outlier analysis can prioritize investigation. It cannot prove
fraud, certify correctness, or replace a risk-limiting audit. A high score means
that a reporting unit differs from nearby units enough to deserve explanation;
it is not itself the explanation.

The intuition is simple. Neighboring reporting units usually resemble each other
because they share local geography, administrative practice, media markets,
commuting patterns, and demographic context. When one unit diverges sharply from
its geographic neighbors in turnout, vote share, residuals, or audit-discrepancy
features, the unit is a candidate for review. The review may find a benign
reason, a data problem, a boundary artifact, or nothing at all.

This paper is a method and framework paper in the W-series. W.01 defines the
shared forensic report contract for investigative, non-certifying anomaly
analytics. W.05 instantiates that contract for spatial neighbor anomalies. It
describes the statistics, inputs, report fields, and intended fixtures. It does
not claim that a W-series forensics crate, fixture suite, or production report
already exists.
