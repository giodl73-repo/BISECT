\begin{abstract}
Election forensic analytics look for unusual patterns in reported results:
outlying precinct swings, turnout and vote-share residuals, batch and scanner
discontinuities, suspicious digit distributions, spatial outliers, abrupt
change points, and clustered audit discrepancies. These methods are valuable
for triage, but they cannot certify an outcome or prove fraud. This paper
defines the W-series, a family adjacent to the RCOUNT count-verification track
but with a deliberately different claim boundary. It surveys the analytic
families that hash-bound RCOUNT packages can feed, specifies a common forensic
report contract every W-series method must satisfy --- source package hashes,
feature set and baseline population, model identity, unit-level scores,
explicit caveats, and a plain investigative-not-certifying statement --- and
fixes the rule that anomaly scores must never masquerade as certification
evidence. It is a framework and contract paper: the analytic methods are
established, the report contract is specified here, and implementation
placement (RCOUNT audit reports versus a later forensics crate) is left as an
explicit open decision.
\end{abstract}
