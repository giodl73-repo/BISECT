\section{Conclusion}

The W-series gives RCOUNT a place to put investigative analytics without letting
them drift into certification. This overview fixes the three things the rest of
the track shares: the analytic families that hash-bound RCOUNT packages can
feed, a single forensic report contract that binds every method to its source
hashes and carries its caveats, and a fixture pattern that rewards detecting
planted anomalies while refusing to escalate explainable ones.

The methods themselves --- turnout and vote-share residuals, batch and scanner
effects, digit and Benford tests, spatial outliers, change-point tests, and
audit-discrepancy clustering --- are the subjects of the W-series method papers
that build on this contract. What this paper establishes is the discipline that
makes them safe to publish: anomaly scores are an investigation queue, bound to
public inputs and stated as investigative, never as proof and never as a
certification pass or fail.
