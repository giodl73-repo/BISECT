\section{Deferral and Boundary}

RCASE is deliberately deferred. The downstream-boundary specification defines
RCASE as the owner of public evidence bundles and claim matrices, while the
layer-access specification places it after the child packages whose hashes and
claim boundaries it reads. That order matters: a composition package cannot
state its own schema honestly until the child packages have stable ways to say
what they prove, what they do not prove, and what their verifiers could not
check.

The first implementation step should therefore not be a broad report generator.
It should be a tiny composition fixture after RCOUNT and RHIST claim boundaries
are stable. That fixture can exercise the child-package index and
\texttt{claims/claim-boundary.json} without pretending that RCASE owns count
arithmetic, unit lineage, or audit method replay.

The boundary rule is the useful part now: RCASE may organize, cite, and
summarize child package claims. It must preserve child hashes, caveats,
conflicts, and unsupported checks. If a child package reports a failed verifier,
a missing source, an unresolved conflict, or a scoped claim, the case layer must
carry that condition forward instead of laundering it into a clean public
sentence.
