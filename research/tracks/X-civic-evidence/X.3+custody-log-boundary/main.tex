\begin{abstract}
Election count reconciliation can show that declared arithmetic is internally
consistent, but it cannot prove the physical history of ballots, devices,
media, seals, or rooms. This paper reserves two downstream civic-evidence
layers for those process records without implementing either one. RLOG is the
future owner of normalized event and status logs: what source logs record.
RCHAIN is the future owner of chain-of-custody evidence: what custody sources
record. Both layers are deliberately deferred until public source artifacts
such as EMS logs, tabulator logs, seal logs, custody forms, and observer
records force concrete schemas. The boundary rule is simple: RLOG can say what
a source log records; RCHAIN can say what a custody source records; neither can
infer custody or correctness from count reconciliation alone.
\end{abstract}
