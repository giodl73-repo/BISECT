\section{Certified and Publicly Staged Execution}
\label{sec:certified-ceremony}

Deterministic replay answers what one program produced. It does not prove that
no permitted cut was better, or that the governing rule was fixed before
officials saw later districts. The certified extension resolves the first gap
with a lexicographic per-cut certificate for population deviation, weighted
boundary, and canonical assignment. Parent-derived child instances bind all
$k-1$ internal decisions to the final one-seat leaves.

The public ceremony addresses the second gap. Genesis commits the rule profile,
root instance, district-count tree, review interval, witness threshold, and
challenge policy. The root cut is released first. Later cuts are released by
canonical tree depth only after the preceding review interval; same-depth cuts
may be reviewed together because their parents are already fixed. Every release
contains self-contained split evidence and extends an immutable hash chain.

An open or halted prefix can be verified without unreleased descendants. A
completed transcript additionally binds the full certified tree. This proves a
specified disclosure and execution process only when external publication
witnesses validate the asserted times. It does not prove that the chosen
objective is normatively correct or that the result is partisan-neutral,
VRA-compliant, or legally adopted.

Future algorithm bakeoffs therefore preserve their ordinary map metrics while
adding a separate procedural scoreboard for proof coverage, prefix
verification, ceremony completion, verifier cost, and tamper resistance.
Historical bakeoffs are not retroactively treated as ceremony evidence.

The executable path-8 bakeoff fixture demonstrates that reporting contract on
four districts. The certified arm records all three internal cuts over two
depth releases and verifies final-tree binding; a matched METIS replay arm is
reported as unsupported for ceremony evidence rather than awarded invented
proof credit. Its timestamps and receipt strings are deterministic test data,
so the fixture demonstrates software behavior, not external publication or
public legitimacy.
