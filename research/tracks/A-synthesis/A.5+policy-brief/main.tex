\begin{mdframed}[style=bluebox]
\textbf{The proposal.} Require every State to publish a reproducible geographic
benchmark, its final congressional plan, every changed block, alternatives,
effects, public comments, and reasons.

\textbf{The North Star.} Replace the heuristic benchmark with an exact,
uniquely selected plan backed by a mathematical proof certificate.
\end{mdframed}

\section*{What We Can Defend Today}

BISECT can produce and verify a partisan-input-excluded geographic benchmark.
It records code, inputs, parameters, assignments, analysis, and reports.

That claim is useful but limited:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item it proves execution, not fairness;
\item it does not establish Voting Rights Act compliance;
\item it does not preserve every community;
\item it does not prove a global optimum; and
\item it does not replace a commission, court, or public process.
\end{itemize}

The model federal bill therefore treats the benchmark as mandatory
\emph{evidence}, not the mandatory final map.

\section*{Why This Is Still A Major Reform}

Today, a final map can hide thousands of choices. Under the proposed standard,
the public receives:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item one precommitted benchmark;
\item one legally adopted final plan;
\item a block-by-block diff;
\item legal authority for every change;
\item a lower-departure lawful alternative for discretionary changes;
\item public and independent review; and
\item a permanent challenge record.
\end{enumerate}

The benchmark cannot be used as a VRA safe harbor. Federally required remedies
are judged against full legal compliance, not proximity to a race-excluded map.

\section*{What The Real Evidence Shows}

The evidence program has become more credible because it reports failures.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Governed NRS v0.3 packages assign all 27,398,654 Census blocks across
the 2000, 2010, and 2020 cycles: 50 States, 435 districts, and 385 recursive
nodes per cycle.
\item Independent package verification found zero omitted or duplicate units,
zero disconnected districts, and zero population-tolerance failures.
\item Only 13 of 1,155 recursive nodes attain an arithmetic exact-population
floor. Exact weighted-boundary and canonical proof coverage remains zero.
\item A precommitted bounded comparison retained all 40 METIS rows. METIS
matched the complete exact primary objective in 30 and the canonical assignment
in 20; the result does not extrapolate to State-scale optimality.
\item A separate Python implementation verifies the three-cycle structural
comparison: 120 node signatures are common to every cycle and 18 States retain
the exact recursive topology in all three.
\item A precommitted county/tract audit publishes and independently verifies
231,765 geography rows. County splits are 1,812 of 3,140 in 2000, 1,819 of
3,142 in 2010, and 1,823 of 3,142 in 2020; tract splits are 17,268 of 65,255,
18,800 of 72,878, and 20,288 of 84,208. These are within-vintage descriptive
counts, not a trend or legal-compliance claim.
\item A separately governed 2020 bakeoff compares NRS v0.3 with official
CD118 on 7,889,194 common land-containing blocks. After State-level label
matching, 4,194,107 block assignments match and 3,695,087 differ
(53.162680\% agreement); NRS splits 1,808 counties and 19,789 tracts, versus
404 and 4,720 in the comparator block projection.
\item The same common-block universe supplies four geometry measures. Both
State- and district-weighted summaries place CD118 above NRS on Polsby--Popper,
exact Reock, and convex-hull ratio and below NRS on Schwartzberg. This is not
original enacted linework and does not identify a fairer, better, or legally
preferred plan.
\item Raw tree hashes reproduce for 149 of 150 State-cycle packages. Maryland
2010 repeats the exact assignment but not one diagnostic move counter, so the
published audit uses its assignment hash and committed substantive node
snapshot and discloses the transport-hash exception.
\item A fresh-clone second-laptop kit can verify committed artifacts or replay
all national assignments. An actual external v0.3 record is still pending.
\item Earlier real Rust and GerryChain ensembles remain archived for Rhode
Island, Iowa, and North Carolina. Their limited scope and convergence failures
remain part of the record.
\item Rhode Island supports a stable low cut-fraction result.
\item Iowa and North Carolina lack enough effective samples for extreme-tail
claims.
\item Rust and GerryChain distributions differ materially in Iowa and North
Carolina.
\item Wisconsin was excluded because a benchmark district was disconnected.
\end{itemize}

These are not marketing defects. They show that the evidence system can reject
its own preferred story.

\section*{The Huntington--Hill North Star}

Huntington--Hill ended operational dispute after Congress selected the method.
Redistricting is harder because it creates geographic regions rather than
allocating seats among fixed States.

The closest equivalent is an \textbf{Exact Canonical Benchmark}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Congress enacts the feasible-set axioms.
\item An exact solver minimizes maximum population deviation.
\item It then minimizes total population deviation.
\item It then minimizes geographic boundary cost.
\item A canonical GEOID assignment vector breaks all remaining ties.
\item A proof certificate establishes that no lexicographically better
assignment exists under the identified enacted instance, model, and profile.
\end{enumerate}

Given the enacted axioms and census inputs, the benchmark would be unique and
mathematically certified. Debate remains over the axioms and lawful
modifications---not over what the mathematics produced.

\begin{mdframed}[style=greenbox]
\textbf{Gold-standard statement:}

``For this enacted instance, model, and profile, this is the unique assignment
selected by the enacted lexicographic objective. The attached certificate
proves feasibility, model-relative optimality, and canonical tie-breaking.''
\end{mdframed}

\section*{Why We Are Not There Yet}

Exact connected redistricting is computationally difficult. NRS v0.3 is an
operational deterministic procedure whose national assignments and tolerance
claims verify; it is not an exact optimizer. Bounded exact split and
recursive-tree certificates, hostile verifier corpora, independent verifiers,
OPB proof requests, and national block RCTX inputs are implemented. They are
foundations, not a complete national exact solver.

The North Star requires:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item scalable block-level exact formulations;
\item compact proof-loggable connectivity constraints;
\item lower bounds matching the incumbent objective;
\item canonical tie-optimality proofs;
\item RoundingSat proof generation and independent VeriPB verification; and
\item demonstrated exact-proof performance from small States to California and
Texas.
\end{itemize}

\section*{The Ask}

\textbf{Do not wait for exact optimization before adopting transparency.}
Adopt the evidence standard now.

\textbf{Do not stop at a heuristic benchmark.}
Fund and test the Exact Canonical Benchmark until it can supply
Huntington--Hill-like operational finality.

\section*{Claim Boundary}

This brief describes an internal candidate standard. It is not enacted law,
external peer review, independent physical-laptop replication, or proof that a
BISECT plan is fair. Block-level operational execution is complete for the
three governed national cycles; exact national boundary/canonical certification
and the full neutral evaluation schedule are not. The exact benchmark remains a
research and engineering goal.
