\begin{abstract}
Gerrymandering is widely condemned by the American public, yet political
reform remains slow. This paper specifies a proposed survey experiment
($N=2{,}400$ respondents) testing perceptions of a
partisan-input-excluded geographic benchmark, commission-drawn maps, and
enacted maps under different information treatments. No respondents have been
recruited, no study has been preregistered, and no empirical result is reported.
Numerical estimates in the worked analysis are synthetic placeholders used to
test table structure, power, and the planned interpretation protocol. The
study must be registered, fielded, archived, and independently reproducible
before any claim about public legitimacy, algorithm aversion, partisan
moderation, or treatment effects may be made.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} redistricting, gerrymandering, survey experiment, algorithm aversion, procedural fairness, public opinion
