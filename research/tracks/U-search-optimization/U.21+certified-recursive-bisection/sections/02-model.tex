\section{Fixed Recursive Model}

For a parent region requiring $k\geq2$ districts, define
\[
k_L=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad k_R=k-k_L.
\]
Every unit is assigned to exactly one child, both children are nonempty and
connected, and each child contains at least as many units as eventual
districts. The solver may choose the cut; it may not alter $(k_L,k_R)$.

Let $P$ be parent population and $P_L$ left-child population. The
seat-ratio-scaled deviation is
\[
\Delta_L=\left|kP_L-k_LP\right|.
\]
The right-child expression is algebraically equal. We retain maximum and total
deviation fields for schema continuity, although total deviation is twice the
maximum for a binary split.

Among feasible assignments, the procedure minimizes lexicographically:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item maximum scaled population deviation;
  \item total scaled population deviation;
  \item weighted boundary cut; and
  \item the canonical binary assignment.
\end{enumerate}

Equal-seat splits have label symmetry, removed by placing canonical unit zero
in the left child. Unequal-seat splits are already oriented by the floor/ceiling
seat counts; forcing unit zero left could exclude the ratio-correct solution
and is therefore prohibited.

Island components use the project's deterministic rule: each non-main-
component unit connects to its nearest same-county unit in the main component,
with statewide fallback. Synthetic bridge edges receive the median
land-boundary weight.
