\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Markov-chain ensemble methods and algorithmic redistricting pursue fundamentally
different goals: the former characterises the space of ``reasonable'' plans in
order to \emph{evaluate} any given plan against a geographic baseline; the
latter produces a single canonical plan that is verifiably optimal by a stated
criterion.  These goals are complementary but often conflated in legal and
academic discourse.  This paper establishes the methodological framework for
the G-series, which situates a deterministic BISECT benchmark within declared
\recom{} ensembles.  We review \gc{}, \recom{}, and Short-Burst methods; introduce
the percentile framework for locating a single plan in an ensemble
distribution; connect ensemble convergence diagnostics ($\rhat$, \ess{},
Hamming autocorrelation) to the \cs{} stopping criterion established in U.1;
and articulate the statutory implications of a \emph{verifiable deterministic
plan} against sampled stochastic plans.  G.1--G.3 instantiate this framework
with a hash-bound three-state tract-level package.  Its real Rust and
GerryChain traces support bounded cut-fraction and 2020 presidential-seat
diagnostics, not national, polygon-compactness, or legal conclusions.
Separately, a preregistered Rhode Island block-graph slice completed four
Wilson and four random-weight Kruskal chains and regenerated normalized traces
exactly.  Its materially different kernel distributions demonstrate why
sampler identity is part of the estimand and why that slice cannot be promoted
to national evidence.  Two separately frozen NH/NM/GA successors retained a
disk-exhaustion failure after five primaries and a compiled-runner identity
failure before the first preflight.  A fresh v3 protocol then passed Stage~0,
completed all six primary State--kernel runs, and regenerated all six exactly
in governed replays.  Under the registered scalar rules, New Hampshire and New
Mexico passed for both kernels, but Georgia did not.  The all-or-nothing v3
gate therefore closed non-converged without retry or extension.  This supports
bounded feasibility, deterministic replay, registered diagnostics, and
State-specific kernel sensitivity, not mixing, sampler equivalence, or
national representativeness.
\end{abstract}
