\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The G-track establishes a rigorous framework for situating the \ar{}
algorithmic redistricting plan within the ensemble landscape.  The central
methodological contribution is the distinction between two goals: ensemble
methods characterize a sampled distribution under a declared profile (Goal
A), while BISECT produces a deterministic plan under a separate declared
construction procedure (Goal B).

These goals are complementary, but the ensemble distribution is conditional
on graph, constraints, proposal kernel, starting plans, and stopping rule. A
benchmark percentile therefore describes one declared comparison; it does not
establish neutrality, legal validity, uniqueness, or exact objective
optimality.

The convergence diagnostics from G.4 ($\rhat$, \ess{}, Hamming autocorrelation)
apply to both ensemble and algorithmic validation.  The \cs{} threshold
$T = 600$ from U.1 translates into an analogue of \ess{} for the seed
sweep: stability across 600 consecutive non-improving seeds is evidence that
the seed space has been adequately explored.

The statutory implication is bounded. Ensemble evidence can locate a declared
benchmark within a declared sampled distribution, but it cannot itself
establish that the benchmark is lawful, neutral, or optimal. The current
G.1--G.3 package measures tract cut fraction and one complete presidential
election in three states; it does not measure polygon compactness, minority
representation, or a national feasible-plan distribution.

G.4 and G.5 develop ensemble diagnostics and mixing analysis. The separately
frozen Rhode Island block-level gate has now completed and regenerated exactly,
but it exposed material Wilson-versus-Kruskal distribution sensitivity. A
separately frozen NH/NM/GA expansion then measured five successful primaries
but failed on the sixth, GA Kruskal, when the host filesystem exhausted its
free space.  The registered stop rule closed the expansion without retry, and
no governed replay ran.  The retained package therefore characterizes a
resource and operational failure envelope; it is not a completed multi-state
ensemble comparison.  Any future expansion requires a new protocol rather
than continuation of the failed schedule.

The current implementation state is more precise than the original G-track
framing. The repository contains a hash-bound real RI/IA/NC package, while the
synthetic and missing-evidence packages preserve the development history.
Accordingly, G.0's methodological claim is package-shaped: ensemble
comparisons are publication-ready only when trace, diagnostic, metric,
election, RPLAN, and RCTX artifacts are hash-bound and replayable, and only
within the package's declared unit, state, election, sampler, and metric
boundaries.

The combined evidence therefore has two non-substitutable resolutions.  The
RI/IA/NC tract package supports its declared tract cut-fraction and election
diagnostics.  The Rhode Island block package supports block-graph feasibility,
registered scalar convergence, deterministic replay, and kernel sensitivity.
Neither package authorizes national block percentiles or legal conclusions.
The partial NH/NM/GA primary package does not add a third completed evidentiary
resolution: without the sixth primary and all exact replays, it supports only
bounded feasibility/resource observations and the registered negative gate
result.
