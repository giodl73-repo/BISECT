\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We compare the tract-level NRS v0.1 BISECT benchmark with four-chain,
2,000-step Rust ReCom and GerryChain ensembles in Rhode Island, Iowa, and North
Carolina.  All inputs, traces, RPLAN/RCTX baselines, diagnostics, and hashes are
archived.  Rhode Island's benchmark cut fraction falls at the 1.35th percentile
in Rust and 1.81st in GerryChain.  Iowa falls at 0.33rd and 0.88th percentiles,
but effective sample sizes are too small for a stable sub-1\% tail claim.
North Carolina falls at 0--0.2\%, yet pooled ESS is only 87--109 and the two
implementations produce materially different cut distributions. Wisconsin was
removed before sampling because its benchmark contained a disconnected
district. These results replace the earlier unsupported six-state percentile
tables and demonstrate why trace custody, diagnostics, and cross-tool
comparison are prerequisites for any extremum claim.
\end{abstract}
