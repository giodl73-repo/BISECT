\section{Boundaries}

The implemented claim boundary is the one stated in the V.18 plan: batch
comparison is useful when ballot-level linkage is unavailable, but large batches
weaken localization and can increase audit workload. RCOUNT can verify that the
batch records are internally consistent and that the taint-product transcript
matches those records. It does not make a ballot-level comparison claim.

The boundary also depends on input trust. The verifier needs reported batch
subtotals that correspond to the canvassed batch summary and hand tallies that
come from the same physical batch. If those records are absent, mislabeled, or
not trustworthy, the replay surface cannot repair them. The missing-hand-tally,
batch-size-drift, missing-sampled-batch, and missing-risk-limit cases therefore
fail or produce explicit boundary transcripts rather than statistical comfort.

Finally, this slice does not call the derivation helper from public source
adapters. The plan keeps that item open until audit reports provide sampled-batch
order. V.18 therefore documents the core replay and verification surface, not a
complete jurisdiction-specific ingestion pipeline.
