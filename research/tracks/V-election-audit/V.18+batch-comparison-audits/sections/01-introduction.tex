\section{Introduction}

Batch-comparison audits sit between ballot-level comparison and aggregate
manual checks. The sampled unit is a whole reported batch: a precinct, scanner
batch, mail batch, or other tabulation unit whose reported contest subtotals can
be compared with a hand tally of the same batch. This is useful when ballots are
only counted or retrievable in batches and ballot-level CVR linkage is not
available.

RCOUNT treats this as a replay contract. The verifier does not accept a free
text statement that a batch was checked. It confirms that the reported winner
and loser subtotals match the canvassed batch summary, that hand-tallied totals
exist for the same selections, and that the declared overstatement equals the
difference between the reported and hand margins. A derived algorithm transcript
then carries the batch overstatement as a taint, encoded as overstatement divided
by reported margin.

This paper covers the implemented V.18 slice. Its claim is deliberately narrow:
when a package publishes trustworthy batch subtotals, hand tallies, and sampled
batch order, the taint-product transcript for \texttt{batch-comparison-v1} can
be regenerated from those public records.
