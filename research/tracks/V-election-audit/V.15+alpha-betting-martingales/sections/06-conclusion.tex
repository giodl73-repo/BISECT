\section{Conclusion}

RCOUNT now has a replayable first ALPHA surface. The verifier reads a single
bounded-mean assertion, its upper bound, a risk limit, ordered assorter values,
and declared bets; then it recomputes the fixed-bet martingale, integer-ppm
$p$-values, and pass-or-continue decision through the shared
\texttt{rcount-stats} primitive. Missing bets and invalid numerical inputs
produce explicit boundary or failure evidence rather than a trusted statistical
verdict.

The implementation is deliberately smaller than the full ALPHA design space. It
establishes the transcript discipline that later adaptive, without-replacement,
stratified, and SHANGRLA-style work will need: the public record must contain
enough information to replay the bet/statistic path, not merely the final risk
number.
