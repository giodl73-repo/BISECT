\section{Introduction}

ALPHA and related betting-martingale audits are usually presented as a flexible
statistical framework: encode an election assertion as a bounded mean, observe a
sample stream, choose a betting parameter, and update a test statistic until the
risk limit is met. The public-verification question is lower level. An observer
must be able to ask whether the declared martingale transcript follows from the
declared assorter values and bets.

RCOUNT therefore treats ALPHA as a replay contract. The verifier does not trust
a reported martingale value or a vendor-exported $p$-value. It reads the audit
record, reconstructs the bounded-mean observations, and recomputes the
nonnegative martingale. Under the null, that martingale never grows in
expectation; large realized values are evidence against the null, and the
running inverse statistic supplies the replayed $p$-value.

This paper covers the implemented V.15 slice. It does not claim to implement
every ALPHA audit design, adaptive bet selector, or sampling mode. It claims
that the current fixed-bet bounded-mean transcript is reproducible when the
package records the assertion, sample order, assorter values, risk limit, and
bet sequence.
