\section{Fixtures}

The positive fixture is a tiny two-candidate run. It reports the winner ahead
three to one, sets the risk limit to ten percent, and supplies a short sequence
of winner observations. The likelihood ratio climbs past the
risk-limit threshold and the replay reports a stop; the declared per-draw
statistics match the recomputed transcript, so the package passes. This is the
toy run exercised by the \texttt{bravo\_algorithm\_statistics\_replay\_passes\_toy\_run}
test.

The continue fixture uses the same reported totals but a sequence of
observations that does not accumulate enough evidence. The likelihood ratio
never reaches the threshold, so the transcript reports continue rather than
stop, and the declared statistic must agree with that non-stopping path. This is
the case covered by
\texttt{bravo\_algorithm\_statistics\_replay\_continues\_when\_threshold\_is\_not\_met}.

The drift negative fixture keeps the observations and totals fixed but edits a
declared per-draw statistic or stop decision. The recomputed transcript diverges
from the declared one, and the package fails: the transcript exposes the
declared and computed values for the offending draw.

The missing-field boundary fixture omits a required reported vote total or the
risk limit. The audit record is preserved, but the BRAVO claim is not replayed:
the verifier emits a boundary transcript that explicitly records the public
evidence as insufficient to recompute the statistic, rather than silently
passing or failing.

Each fixture pair separates the two failure modes that matter for a
ballot-polling audit: an audit that does not actually stop under its declared
observations, and an audit that cannot be replayed at all from the public
record.
