\section{Introduction}

SHANGRLA is useful because it changes the unit of an election audit claim. A
contest outcome is no longer treated as one opaque stopping rule. It is reduced
to assertions, and each assertion is evaluated by an assorter: a bounded value
assigned to a ballot, CVR, comparison record, batch, or other sampled unit. When
the assorter mean is greater than $1/2$, the assertion supports the reported
outcome.

That abstraction is deliberately broader than any one audit method. BRAVO,
Kaplan-Markov comparison audits, ALPHA betting martingales, and some ranked
choice audit paths can all be described as tests over assertion-level evidence.
The RCOUNT V.16 slice therefore focuses on the assertion and assorter contract
itself. A package should say which assertion is being tested, which assorter is
being used, which sample unit received which assorter value, and what declared
risk evidence or decision is attached to that sequence.

The implemented claim is modest. RCOUNT does not yet recompute a SHANGRLA mean
test from this method id, and it does not claim that a reported outcome has been
risk-limited by V.16 alone. It verifies that the declared assertion and assorter
record is reproducible as a public record shape: ids are linked, values are
bounded, and the half-average layer is ready for a later ALPHA or other mean
replay.
